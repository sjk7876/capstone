Volleyball serve analysis is typically performed through manual video review, which is slow, subjective, and provides limited quantitative feedback. This work presents a lightweight, single-camera computer vision system for automated volleyball serve analysis from standard baseline video. The pipeline detects and tracks the volleyball with a fine-tuned YOLOv8 object detector combined with SORT-based tracking and trajectory post-processing. Serve contact and landing frames are estimated automatically from smoothed ball trajectories using size- and motion-based heuristics, while court geometry is recovered through manual annotation of visible court corners and homography-based calibration. This enables projection of serve trajectories and landing locations into real-court coordinates, supporting accuracy, consistency, and zone-based analysis. Evaluation on a privately collected dataset of annotated serves shows a mAP50--95 of 0.911 for ball detection and landing-frame estimates within approximately one frame of human-labeled ground truth. These results demonstrate that accurate, quantitative volleyball serve analysis is achievable using only a single camera and lightweight computer vision techniques.
